Title
Transition from Ischial Containment to Sub-Ischial Transfemoral Socket Design
Date of event being reflected upon
Wednesday 22 October 2025
Introduction
I had the opportunity to design and fabricate a sub-ischial transfemoral socket for a
patient who had previously been using an ischial containment socket for over 6years .
This experience allowed me to apply my knowledge of transfemoral socket design in a
practical clinical situation and further develop my understanding of the differences
between ischial containment and sub-ischial socket designs.
What event or topic are you reflecting on?
Designing and fabricating a sub-ischial transfemoral socket for apatient who had been
using an ischial containment socket for over 6 years,and comparing the two desings
effect on comfort,suspension,control and gait.
Would you call this a positive or challenging event?
Based of from the experience its a positive story,the patient reported improved comfort at
the preineal region,especially during sitting,gait,and hip stability,better prosthetic control
was assessed and outcome measures (PLUS-M™and Onform gait analysis) supported
the process.it improved patience confidence with device and shifted my practice towards
patient-centered and evidence based decisions.
What happened?
I incorporated the patient’s clinical anatomical findings and relevant clinical findings into
the socket design and fabrication process by using NU FlexSIV and NU FlexSIS socket
designs . During the test fitting, I assessed the socket in the various plans of the body,I
observed the patient’s comfort, suspension, residual-limb movement within the socket,
pelvic stability and gait pattern. I also obtained honest feedback from the patient
regarding areas of pressure, comfort while sitting and their overall perception of the new
socket compared with the previous ischial containment design.
The fitting process reinforced the importance of making incremental modifications based
on both clinical assessment and patient feedback. I learned that small changes to the
socket shape, trimlines and pressure distribution can have a significant effect on comfort,
stability and gait. The experience also improved my confidence in evaluating whether the
socket was providing adequate containment without unnecessarily restricting the patient.
Looking back
This case improved my practical understanding of sub-ischial transfemoral socket design

establishment of best practices tailored specifically for this demographic. The conversation
underscores the critical understanding that treating children necessitates a unique
approach, one that is influenced by factors such as growth, family involvement, and
adherence to treatment protocols.

is joined by Kiley Armstrong, MPO, CPO, Research Prosthetist-Orthotist at Northwestern
University’s Feinberg School of Medicine, and Juan Cave II, MSPO, CPO, FAAOP,

share insights from their professional journeys into the fields of O&P and research, discuss

practitioners who are keen to contribute to research initiatives. The episode underscores the

clinical practice with research efforts.
Description of the learning activity – what did it consist of?

and Juan Cave II, both esteemed professionals in the field of O&P research. The discussion
centres on the importance of conducting clinically relevant research within the O&P
community. Throughout the episode, the participants explore how clinicians can influence
and shape research that is meaningful and applicable to real-world practice. Kiley and Juan
recount their personal experiences and the significant projects they have undertaken,
providing listeners with valuable insights into the research process. Furthermore, they offer
practical advice for practitioners who wish to engage in research, highlighting the necessity
of collaboration and networking to foster a more integrated approach between clinical
practice and research initiatives.

Knowledge and Skills Framework
1: Communication Communication has many forms and is a two-way process. It involves identifying what others are
communicating as well as communicating yourself and the development of effective relationships.
2: Personal and People Development This dimension is about developing yourself and contributing to the
development of others through both formal structured and informal ad-hoc methods.
3: Health Safety and Security This dimension focuses on maintaining the health, safety and security of everyone in
the organisation and anyone who comes into contact with it. It includes tasks that are undertaken as a routine part of
work, such as moving or handling.
4: Service Improvement This dimension is about improving services in the interests of the users of those services and
the public as a whole. The services might be services for the public (patients, clients and carers) or be services that
support the smooth running of the organisation (such as finance, estates). The services might be single or multi-agency
and uni or multi-professional.
5: Quality This dimension relates to maintaining high quality in all areas of work and practice, including the important
aspects of effective team working. Quality can be supported using a range of different approaches including: codes of
conduct and practice, evidence-based practice, guidelines, legislation, protocols, procedures, policies, standards and
systems.
6: Equality and Diversity It is the responsibility of every person to act in ways that support equality and diversity.
Equality and diversity is related to the actions and responsibilities of everyone - users of services including patients,
clients and carers; work colleagues; employees, people in other organisations; the public in general.
